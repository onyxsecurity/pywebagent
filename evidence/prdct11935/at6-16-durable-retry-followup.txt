## 3. WHAT THAT COST, observed
   During round-26 verification the kind node self-tainted with disk-pressure and postgres-1 was
   EVICTED, so every in-flight object failed with ConnectionRefusedError to postgres-rw. The consumer
   behaved correctly per message — it abandoned rather than acked — but the budget ran out: the queue's
   $DeadLetterQueue deadLetterMessageCount 29 -> 57, i.e. roughly 28 real scanner objects permanently parked with
   DeadLetterReason MaxDeliveryCountExceeded. Captured by the verifier under evidence/verify-r26/.
   The growth, as a table so the label carries both sides:
     deadLetterMessageCount | moment
     -----------------------+------------------
     29                     | before the outage
     57                     | after the outage

## 4. WHY IT IS ALSO A SETTLEMENT-INVENTORY GAP
   A broker-side MaxDeliveryCountExceeded park runs NO application code, so no app-side counter moves —
   it is item 4 of the label-less exits this PR's own inventory now reports (at6-01). An operator
   watching asset_ingestion_* series sees nothing at all while objects are being lost. That is why the
   two findings are filed together.

## 5. SCOPE — deliberately not fixed here
   consumer_pool is shared with the SQS sibling, so changing retry semantics is a behavioural change on
   every cloud and belongs in its own change. The existing constraints must survive any fix: absence of
   a successful write is never a delete instruction, and a genuinely poison message must still park
   rather than loop forever (which is exactly what the current short budget is good at).
