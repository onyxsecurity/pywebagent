=== 5. HOW THE BURST WAS SIZED — against the poll interval, with the numbers
================================================================================
    The known trap: a single replica drains a small burst before anything polls, so the depth is never
    observed and a real arm reads as a dead one. Sized against three measured quantities:

      (i)   HPA sync period ................ 15s (kube-controller-manager default; confirmed by the 15s
                                             spacing of the four SuccessfulRescale events in section 6)
      (ii)  KEDA pollingInterval ........... 30s (spec, section 1)
      (iii) one replica's drain rate ....... 0.9 msg/s, MEASURED in this very window: over 02:55:03Z->02:55:21Z
                                             the deployment still had exactly 1 READY replica and the metric
                                             fell 303 -> 287, i.e. 16 messages in 18s.
      (iv)  scale threshold ................ HPA scales out when metric/target exceeds the 10% tolerance:
                                             with 1 replica and averageValue target 10, that needs
                                             ActiveMessageCount >= 12.
      (v)   receiver lock hiding ........... the consumer's config is max_messages: 10 / concurrency: 2, so up
                                             to 10 delivered messages are LOCKED and therefore NOT counted in
                                             ActiveMessageCount at any instant.

    Minimum burst = 12 (threshold) + 10 (locked, hidden) + 0.9 * 30 (drained during one KEDA poll interval)
                  = ~49 messages.
    Chosen burst = 300 (~6x that floor). The margin was deliberate: it keeps the depth above threshold across
    several consecutive polls rather than one lucky sample, and it drives the trigger through more than a
    single scale step. The measured hold time above the threshold was ~2m20s (02:54:58Z -> 02:57:23Z), i.e.
    ~9 HPA syncs, not one.
